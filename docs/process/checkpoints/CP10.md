# CP10 — Pre-freeze evaluation fixes (analysis only)

All new numbers (`results/cp10.json`, `results/cp10_world.json`, `results/iforest.json`) are **post-hoc descriptive analyses of the frozen version 1 method, added after
CP7**. No detector, estimator, threshold, simulation code or CP7 result changed. New seeds were
declared in `docs/eval_protocol.md` addendum A (commit e42dd50) before any run. The primary detector
`bayes` is now also called `lr_band`: a likelihood-ratio test with a two-sided abstention band.
`tests/test_lr_band.py` proves the two names give identical output.

## Implemented
- `shahid/records.py`: per-episode and per-group records (evidence, verdict, truth, size class) for 46 families. `--verify` checks them against the published aggregates.
- `shahid/cp10.py`: R3 harm, operator load, outcome split, base rates and the P(real outage) curve, continuous boundary fits on three axes with a (seed, day)-cluster bootstrap, size classes, the safety-limit table, paired coverage differences, diversity ranges and the gap diagnostic. `shahid/cp10_world.py`: spread across simulated worlds.
- `shahid/iforest.py`: Isolation Forest baseline (Task 6).
- `configs/escalation.yaml`: R3 replaced by a provisional rule with an 8-hour backlog re-check (DESIGN CHOICE).
- `docs/process/phase2_candidates.md`. Assumptions A-LATENCY, A-PREVALENCE and A-ZONES-REAL. Limitations 17–21. Results document and ledger updated (CP10-*). Superseded statements are marked, not deleted.

## Measured
- **CP10-REC**: 264 aggregate checks of the regenerated records against CP5, CP6 and CP7, 0 mismatches.
- **T0 (R3)**: version 1 R3 automatically closed real outages as comms failures in 0.9–1.8% of outages nominally. That rose to 4.6% at 2% transformer gain error, 8.3% for simultaneous events, 12.2% at 5 kW and 24.1% at 10 kW of load-shaped unmetered energy.
- **T1 (operator load)**: at 1% loss, 10.2–11.3 human items per transformer-day, over 99% of them "uncertain" 1–2-meter silences; outage approvals 0.08 per day. At 10% loss, 65.7–67.8 per day. **Ordinary packet loss floods the operator.** An undetected outage is counted as missed (confirmed).
- **T2 (base rates)**: 32–40 false outage verdicts per transformer-year, all on 1–3-meter silent sets. P(real outage given an outage verdict) is 0.016 at an assumed 1 real outage per transformer-year. No verified outage-frequency source was found within 30 minutes: an Ofgem 2010–11 report could not be read, an EPRI page had no figures, and an MDPI page returned 403. TODO, no number.
- **T3 (boundary)**: share at P(correct) = 0.90 is 0.160 (0.124–0.207) nominal, 0.083 (0.059–0.119) held-out and 0.114 (0.080–0.165) dev-test. The signal-to-noise axis is the most consistent: about 6 nominal, 4.9 held-out.
- **T4 (size)**: single-connection coverage 0.16–0.23, whole-feeder 0.71–0.97. Dedicated whole-feeder families on new seeds: outage recall 0.689 (dev) and 0.637 (held-out), missed 1.1%.
- **T5 (equal safety)**: only lr_band (coverage 0.663) and ztest (0.602) meet the version 1 limits. Share never meets the false-alarm budget. Held-out paired coverage: lr_band − ztest +0.087 (0.049–0.131); lr_band − share +0.004 (−0.042–0.047).
- **T6 (Isolation Forest)**: detects that something happened (AUC 0.95) but not which cause (AUC 0.41). On held-out it calls 92% of comms failures outages.
- **T7 (diversity)**: nominal coverage 0.599 sits in a range of 0.484–0.663 over d = 0…1.
- **T8 (gap)**: hypothesis **supported**. Transformer gain is drawn once per scenario. The gap residual correlates with load change (−0.71 for gain error, 0.56 for load-shaped unmetered, 0.14 nominal), and a load-dependent fit would cut its spread by 26%, 21% and 2%.
- **T10b (worlds)**: on three more worlds, abstention 0.397 (world 0: 0.401), balanced 0.583–0.586 across all four, false outage 0, missed 0.013–0.017.

## Failed or surprising
- The operator-load and base-rate results are the most important new findings. Labelled-episode precision of 1.000 coexists with about 40 false outage verdicts per transformer-year, all on tiny silent sets. Under the escalation table a human sees about 11 items a day from ordinary data loss.
- The share boundary differs between grids by a factor of 1.4, while the signal-to-noise boundary transfers better. The old "16% / 32%" values were artefacts of doubling bin widths.
- Isolation Forest is excellent at "something happened" and useless at "why". This supports the physics-based design.
- Two numbers I wrote during the audit and this checkpoint were wrong and were caught by checking against result files before commit: an outage transformer value in AUDIT §6, and a placeholder commit hash in the ledger.
- The CP10 analysis first ran slowly because the escalation table was re-read from disk for every group. It was stopped on request and rerun after that fix.
- Task 10a (full `python -m shahid.reproduce` in a fresh clone, compared file by file with `python -m shahid.verify_repro --all`): NOT RUN. Estimated 4–5 hours of heavy CPU; left for an unattended night run on the user's request after the earlier overheating. The headline stages were already verified identical in a fresh clone (AU-08).

## Assumptions remaining
- New: A-LATENCY (timely data delivery), A-PREVALENCE (artificial event mix, real outage frequency unknown), A-ZONES-REAL (all single-line openings are plausible outage zones).
- Unchanged and most consequential: P7 (transformer meter) and A-DIV (diversity).

## Decision and recommendation
- Keep the version 1 method frozen. Report it with coverage, the diversity range, size classes and the base-rate caveat.
- The Phase 2 priority is operator load and false outages on tiny silent sets (candidates 1 and 2), then a load-dependent gap (candidate 3).
- R3 is now provisional with a backlog re-check (DESIGN CHOICE, not evaluated). The team should confirm it.
