Version 1 (detector, estimator, uncertainty model, thresholds, simulation, CP7 results) is unchanged
and remains the registered result. No new simulation or power flow was run. Every policy row other
than version 1 is a "post-hoc variant, not pre-registered". Protocol: `docs/eval_protocol.md`

  - for each row: outcome tables on three grids, operator load by type, false outages per

  - the small-group residual analysis, break-even ratios with a paired bootstrap, and the headline
    ranges.

  - The reproducibility counts moved off the allowlist into `results/repro_summary.json`

## Measured
**Task 1, one matching rule (CP12-T1).**
- The selection metric is unchanged: branch and feeder outages without real-time attention on CP5
  validation. So the addendum C choice of the gate stands.
- The CP11 numbers that change are listed in `docs/RESULTS.md` §6.6. The largest change
  is in the simultaneous outage-and-comms cell: version 1 auto-closed 22 outages, not 93. The
  onset-only rule had credited comms groups to outages.
- **The whole-feeder "never detected" rise under version 1.1 is real, not a matching artefact.**
  CP11's column merged three outcomes. Under the gate, 16 (development) and 17 (held-out) of 350
  whole-feeder outages are closed with no verdict and no human. Each is a feeder of 1–3 meters whose
  power returns within the 1 h WATCH, without the reading of the first silent interval ever
  arriving. The 2 genuinely undetected outages per grid are missed by version 1 as well.

**Task 2, components (CP12-T2a, T2b).**
- The gate alone produces all of the false-outage reduction:
  - semiurb5 32.7 → 0.2 per transformer-year;
  - urban6 33.3 → 0.4;
  - rural3 26.9 → 1.0.
- It also produces all of the fault-free load reduction: uncertain items 11.26 → 0.03 per day at 1%
  loss on the held-out grid.
- The re-check alone changes neither.
- At 1 h the re-check sends 0.729 of genuine held-out feeder comms failures to a human for
  confirmation. At 2 h the share is 0.523, at 4 h 0.074, and at 8 h 0.000.
- It catches all 57 outages that version 1 closed as comms under 10 kW load-shaped unmetered energy.
  It catches 35 of them in real time at 1 h, and none at 4 h.
- Simulated comms failures last 1–4 h (median 2.5 h). So the 8 h result is a property of the
  simulator, and every re-check figure depends on A-COMMS.
- Human items are reported in three types everywhere (§6.2 of the results document).

**Task 3, tiny groups (CP12-T3).**
- On fault-free validation data, single-meter residuals are not under-dispersed. 0.954 (development)
  and 0.952 (held-out) fall inside the nominal 90% interval.
- The deep lower tail (z < −4) is lighter than the model's: 0.0024 and 0.0047 observed, against
  0.0081 under the model.
- Single meters with a clearly positive expectation read near zero in 0.0022 of intervals, against
  0.0510 implied by the model.
- Two-meter groups fall below the interval more often than nominal: 0.070 and 0.096.
- **The hypothesis that predictive uncertainty is understated for very small groups is refuted for
  single meters and partly supported for two-meter groups.** False outage verdicts are deep-tail
  residuals at roughly the model's own rate. Their count follows the number of tiny random-loss
  groups under a 50/50 prior: a base-rate effect. The resolution-limit statement now carries this
  scope everywhere.

**Task 4, break-even (CP12-T4).** On held-out whole-feeder events with a 50% outage mix, version 1
needs these extra human reviews per wrong automatic verdict avoided:

| Compared with | Extra reviews per wrong verdict avoided (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| naive baseline | 2.44 (1.97–3.08) |
| step baseline | 0.98 (0.86–1.12) |
| count baseline | 0.59 (0.52–0.67) |
| Isolation Forest | 0.58 (0.51–0.65) |

Version 1 dominates the structure baseline: fewer reviews and fewer wrong verdicts. The ratio
against naive grows with the outage share of the mix (results §8).

## Failed or surprising
- **A bug of my own in the first CP12 run.** The Task 1 comparison fed communication episodes into
  the outage-attention summary, which showed CP5 branch and feeder no-attention jumping to about
  half. It was caught by comparing row by row, and fixed before anything was recorded; only one CP5
  episode actually changes match.
- **The gate silently loses some small-feeder outages.** If the meters come back without the first
  silent reading, which is the outage signature under A-COMMS, the gate closes the case without a
  verdict. This is a design flaw of the post-hoc gate (phase 2 candidate 9).
- **The re-check has no evidential timeout in this simulator.** Short timeouts turn most genuine comms
  failures into human items. Long ones catch outages only after they end. 8 h never fires.
- **A hand-typed count was wrong.** CP11-REPRO said 12 extra-family files differed; the comparison
  file shows 11 parquet files. It is corrected and now backed by `results/repro_summary.json`.
- **Rounding:** a half-width exactly at a power of ten made the generator and the checker disagree.
  The generator now settles on the decimals that remain valid after rounding the bounds.

## Assumptions remaining
- New: **A-COMMS-DURATION**, simulated comms failures last 1–4 h; every re-check figure depends on
  it. Also **A-NEARZERO**, the Task 3 thresholds (a DESIGN CHOICE).
- The gate (fewer than 3 meters, 1 h WATCH) remains a DESIGN CHOICE selected under a declared
  deviation, and is not operator-validated.

## Decision and recommendation
**Present in the proposal:**
- **Version 1, as the registered result.** Quote its headline ranges, never one grid alone, and
  always with coverage.
- **Version 1 + meter-count gate, as a clearly labelled post-hoc option with both sides of its
  trade-off.**
  - It removes nearly all false outage verdicts (about 33 → under 1 per transformer-year) and almost
    all fault-free human items.
  - In exchange, more outages lose real-time attention (whole-feeder held-out 0.086 → 0.131; rural
    0.091 → 0.212).
  - Some single-meter-feeder outages are closed with no human at all.
  - The evidence supports showing it as a configurable policy, not as an improvement.

**Do not present as a result:**
- **The comms re-check, at any timeout.** Its cost and benefit are set by the simulated failure
  durations (1–4 h) and by A-COMMS, not by evidence: 1–2 h hands most genuine comms failures to a
  human, 4 h catches outages only after they end, and 8 h is vacuous here. Mention it only as a
  design idea that needs real failure-duration data (phase 2 candidate 10).
- **"Version 1.1" (gate + re-check) as one bundled policy.** Its single row hid that one component
  does the work and the other mostly adds cost.
